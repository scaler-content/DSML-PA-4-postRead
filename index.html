The method

What Is Root Cause Analysis?

Root cause

The core issue — the underlying cause — of a problem.

Root cause analysis

A systematic process for identifying the underlying causes of a problem: break it into smaller parts, examine each in detail, and find the causes contributing to it.

You already do this every day
🤒
Sick at work? A doctor finds the root cause.
🚗
Car won't start? A mechanic finds the root cause.
📉
Business underperforming? You find out why.
🧩 Analogy
🕵️
RCA = detective work
A detective doesn't accuse the first person they see
They establish the facts, rule out false alarms, then interrogate every suspect — eliminating each until one can't be cleared. RCA runs the same playbook on a broken metric. (The everyday version: a doctor diagnosing symptoms, a mechanic tracing a fault.)

The root cause

Three goals · three questions

What's the problem?

Define the issue by its impact on overall goals.

Why did it happen?

List potential causal factors; determine the root cause.

How to prevent it?

Select the best solution to prevent any negative impact recurring.

Quiz 1 · The goal of RCA
A team notices checkout errors, adds a "retry payment" button so users can try again, and moves on. From an RCA perspective, what have they mostly failed to do?
Answer: B. RCA's goals are to discover the root cause, fix/learn from it, and prevent recurrence. A retry button papers over the symptom (failed payments) without asking why payments fail — so the underlying cause remains and the problem will return.
The repeatable steps

Life Cycle of an RCA

Four stages — and it's the exact CRIED spine from Lecture 1, now run at full depth. Tap each to expand.

C
Clarify
Pin down the problem
▼
  • Ask questions to get clarity on the problem
  • Clarify terms and set parameters for discussion
  • Outline the approach you'll follow
R
Rule Out
Kill the boring explanations
▼
  • List high-level causes; discard out-of-scope ones
  • Check the underlying data is accurate
  • Rule out technical issues, glitches, bugs, outliers
  • List observations and start diagnosing
I
Internal Factors
Look inside the company
▼
  • Any recent features / products launched?
  • Any recent changes by other teams?
  • Slice & dice the data by user demographics (= Segmentation from TROPiCS)
E
External Factors
Look at the world outside
▼
  • Any bad PR or controversy?
  • Any change in user behaviour or customer trends?
  • Macroeconomic or geographical changes?
  • Competitor analysis

Note: capture and report all findings, no matter how small or insignificant they may seem.

Quiz 2 · The RCA life cycle
Midway through an RCA on a sales dip, an analyst confirms the tracking pixel wasn't double-counting and the data pipeline had no failures. Which stage is this, and why does it come early?
Answer: B. Checking for technical issues, glitches, outliers and inaccurate data is the Rule Out stage. It comes early because a "drop" that is really a logging bug would send the whole analysis chasing a ghost — same lesson as "Rule Out" in CRIED.
The case

Myntra — a 5% → 3% Drop in Confirmed Orders

Problem statement

Myntra has observed a decline from 5% to 3% (i.e. 2%) in confirmed orders over the last 3 days. Perform a Root Cause Analysis and diagnose the issue.

First, the evidence source: clickstream data

Clickstream is the information collected as a user browses — unique vs repeat visitor, search terms, landing page, time on page, features clicked, cart adds/removes, where they go next, when they hit back.

🧩 Analogy
📹
Clickstream = store CCTV
Clickstream is CCTV footage of your store
One shopper's walk tells you little. Play back thousands of tapes and you see exactly which aisle people wander into, where they double back, and where they drop the basket and leave. Aggregated clickstream is how you spot the leak.

Drop-off / bounce points to fix
User info

Search terms, pages landed on, features used, cart adds/removes → actionable insight.

User routes

The different paths visitors take to reach a page or make a purchase.

Trends & UX

Fast exits flag poorly-optimised pages; spot UX shortcomings to fix.

Digital marketing

Which ads/campaigns drive traffic & conversion, and when they work best.

Establishing assumptions (the Clarify step)

Before counting anything, agree on exactly what each term means.

Cart addition

A user adds an item from the product page to their 'cart'; they can later view it and add/remove items.

Session

The browsing period between a login and the respective logout. One session can hold multiple orders or cart additions.

Conversion

The metric Myntra watches is orders per session:

Orders per session = orders placed in a day ÷ total sessions that day

One order can have multiple items.

Bounce session

A session where the user landed only on a single page (homepage) and left.

Bounce rate = bounce sessions in a day ÷ total sessions that day

Quiz 3 · Backtracking the funnel
Sessions are flat, bounce rate is steady at 10%, and product-page ratings, delivery times and discounts are all unchanged — yet confirmed orders fell 2%. What does this pattern tell you?
Answer: B. Every earlier stage checks out as unchanged, so by elimination the leak must be downstream. Backtracking narrows the 2% drop to the last three stages — exactly where the investigation should now focus.
Ask clarifying questions

Interrogate Every Suspect

Now narrow the why. Each question rules a cause in or out. Tap to reveal what the data said.

External · competitors

Has a competitor (e.g. Ajio) announced a sale or campaign?

Ruled out (tentatively)

Limited visibility, but customer chatter and competitor social handles show nothing obvious. Can't fully rule out hidden targeting — but no evidence.

Demographic · region

Is the decline concentrated in a particular region or city?

Ruled out

No location effect — the impact is spread across many different places.

Demographic · gender/age

Is it tied to a specific gender or age group?

Ruled out

Uniformly distributed across all genders and age groups — no significant deviation.

Macro · supply

Any partnership change, merchant backing out, or stock-outs?

Ruled out

No such event recorded; enough stock for the majority of listed products.

Product · changes

Any recent changes or upgrades on the application?

Lead — door stays open

Yes. Certain upgrades were deployed to the app in the last few days.

🚪 Every external and demographic cause gets ruled out — until one door stays open: a recent app upgrade. RCA is as much about confidently eliminating suspects as finding the culprit.
Diagnose the root cause

Follow the One Surviving Lead

The app upgrade survived every question. Follow it — question by question — until the evidence lands.

Q1 · Any change to the "Add to Cart" button or the Checkout page?

The version upgrade changed the complete shopping flow — the checkout process was reworked for a "seamless" experience. But the Add to Cart button's placement and design were left untouched.

Q2 · Any bug reports for the changes?

Not too many — the update launched only a few days ago, so reports are still thin.

Q3 · Are we missing any leading payment merchants?

No — Myntra facilitates Paytm, PhonePe & GPay and most leading merchants. Clear on this front.

Q4 · Are the payment gateways working properly?

The break

We have received complaints from customers who faced issues while trying to make payment during checkout.

Payment-gateway reliability

Third-party data comparing gateway reliability shows the pattern clearly: 3 of 5 partnering banking services are down.

🔴 Gateway 1 · high downtime
🔴 Gateway 2 · high downtime
🔴 Gateway 3 · high downtime
🟢 Gateway 4 · operational
🟢 Gateway 5 · operational
✅ Conclusion — root cause found
  • 3 of 5 banking services providing payment options are experiencing frequent issues & high server downtime this week
  • This validates the customer complaints about incomplete transactions and payment failures
  • If a payment fails multiple times, customers won't proceed — for fear of losing money — so confirmed orders fall automatically
  • But users are still adding to carts normally → no critical issue with the app itself → things should return to normal once the server issues resolve
Quiz 4 · Reading the root cause
The data shows 3 of 5 payment gateways with high downtime, yet users are still adding items to carts at normal rates. Why is that second fact reassuring for Myntra?
Answer: B. Healthy cart additions mean users still want to buy and the funnel up to payment works — so the loss isn't a product or demand problem. The break is external (bank gateway downtime), which points to a clear, temporary fix and a likely recovery once the servers stabilise.
Interview corner

Practice Questions

Frame your own answer first — then reveal the approach.

Q1 — A food-delivery app's orders dropped 15% this week. Walk me through your RCA.
Full RCA
Clarify: orders how defined? 15% vs what baseline? Which regions/segments?
Rule Out: tracking/logging bug, one-off outlier day, pipeline failure.
Internal: recent app release, pricing/menu change, slice by platform & segment.
External: competitor promo, weather/holiday, delivery-partner shortage.
Diagnose: follow the surviving lead down the funnel to the failing stage; recommend fix + prevention.
Q2 — Signups jumped 30% overnight. Is that good news? How do you check?
Rule Out first
Rule Out: a spike can be a tracking double-count or bot/fraud sign-ups — verify before celebrating.
Segment: one channel, region or device? A single source spiking hints at a campaign or a bug.
Confirm value: do the new signups activate & convert, or vanish? An "up" metric deserves the same scrutiny as a "down" one.
Q3 — Why is "Rule Out" always the step you do before hunting real causes?
Concept
A "drop" that's really a logging bug sends the whole analysis chasing a ghost — wasting days on a cause that doesn't exist.
Cheapest, fastest to check: data accuracy, glitches, outliers. Eliminate the boring explanation before the expensive investigation.
Q4 — After you find the root cause, what makes an RCA actually "complete"?
Prevention
Not just naming the cause — the three goals: discover it, fix/learn from it, and prevent recurrence.
A retry button that hides failing payments isn't an RCA. Add gateway monitoring/alerts and a fallback so the same break can't silently cost orders again.
